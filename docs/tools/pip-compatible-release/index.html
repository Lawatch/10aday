pip ~= “compatible release” and PEP 440 version specifiers: exact ranges for requirements.txt

Short answer: ~=1.4.2 means ≥1.4.2 and ==1.4.* (so <1.5.0); ~=1.4 means ≥1.4 and ==1.* (so <2.0). The last number you wrote may increase. ~=1 is invalid: compatible release needs at least two numbers. ==1.4 is exact (it matches 1.4.0 too, because PEP 440 pads with zeros), ==1.4.* is the wildcard, and a version without an operator (requests 2.31) is not a valid specifier. Multiple clauses are joined with a comma, and there is no “or”.

Rules of thumb for requirements.txt and pyproject.toml

  • For a library that follows semver, ~=1.4 (any 1.x from 1.4) is the usual choice; ~=1.4.2 allows bug-fix releases only.
  • Python has no caret: ^1.4 is a Poetry extension, rejected by pip and by the [project] table of pyproject.toml.
  • Coming from Ruby? ~= is your ~>, except that it needs two numbers. Coming from npm? See ~> vs ~= vs ~.

Common questions

What does ~= mean in requirements.txt?

It is the PEP 440 “compatible release” operator. ~=1.4.2 = ≥1.4.2, ==1.4.* (<1.5.0). ~=1.4 = ≥1.4, ==1.* (<2.0). It equals Ruby's ~> and Composer's ~, but not npm's ~ when you write two numbers.

What is the difference between ==1.4 and ==1.4.*?

==1.4 matches only 1.4 (and 1.4.0, which is the same version under PEP 440). ==1.4.* matches every 1.4.x release: ≥1.4.0 <1.5.0.

Why is ~=1 invalid?

PEP 440 requires at least two release numbers with ~=, because it drops the last one to build the prefix. Use ~=1.0 (≥1.0, <2.0) or ==1.* instead.

How do I say "1.x but not 1.4.1" in pip?

Join clauses with a comma: >=1.0, <2.0, !=1.4.1. Every clause must hold. pip has no “or” operator; || is rejected.
