The surprises: + is a text prefix, and betas count

1.+ does not mean “the newest stable 1.x”. Gradle keeps every version whose text starts with 1., then takes the highest one. Betas and release candidates are included: if 1.11-rc1 is the highest, you get it. Without the dot, 1+ also takes 10.0 and 11.2. Ranges have their own rule: in [1.0,2.0), anything that starts with 2.0 is left out, so 2.0-rc1 is skipped (Maven would take it). How Gradle orders versions

Gradle cuts a version into parts at ., -, _ and +, and also where digits meet letters (1.0a1 = 1, 0, a, 1). Parts are compared one by one. Numbers are compared as numbers (1.10 > 1.9). A number beats a word (1.0.1 > 1.0-beta). A few words have a fixed rank: dev < any other word < rc < snapshot < final < ga < release < sp. Other words are compared letter by letter and case matters: capitals come first, so 1.0-M1 < 1.0-alpha1 and 1.0-Beta1 < 1.0-alpha1. A version with an extra word part is lower than without it (1.0-final < 1.0, even 1.0-sp1 < 1.0); an extra number part makes it higher (1.0 < 1.0.0). The separators do not count: 1.0-1 and 1.0.1 rank the same.
